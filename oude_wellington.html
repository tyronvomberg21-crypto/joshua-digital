The Estate

Two centuries of Boland farm life

Oude Wellington sits on Bainskloof Road at the foot of the Hawequa mountains — a genuine working estate, not a themed one. Vines run down to the dam, Arabian horses graze the paddocks, alpacas and parrots keep the gardens company, and the old gabled homestead has been welcoming travellers since the 18th century.

Days here are simple: breakfast in the historic farm kitchen, a swim under the oaks, a massage at the in-house spa, pétanque or giant chess on the lawn — and in the evening, the walk down the brick lane to the restaurant, glass in hand.

Hosts speak English, Afrikaans, German and Chinese — a rare welcome in four languages.

Brandy & Wine

Dr. Schumacher Potstill Brandy

The estate distils its own five-year potstill brandy the Cognac way — copper still, French oak barriques, patience — and it carries the owner's name. Oude Wellington is a member of the SA Brandy Foundation's Brandy Homes route, and tastings happen where the spirit is actually made.

Estate wine and brandy tastings, cellar and distillery tours — book at the homestead.

Few places in the Winelands let you taste a brandy ten metres from the still it came off. This is one of them.

The Restaurant

Wellington's table, inside the still house

The estate restaurant serves a blackboard bistro menu — country cooking with a South African twist — inside the working 1795 distillery. It ranks among the very best tables in Wellington, and locals guard the Thursday steak special jealously.

Lunch and dinner Tuesday to Saturday, and the famous buffet lunch on Sundays. Residents stroll home along the brick lane afterwards; everyone else wishes they could.
